📊 Why This Is Game #1
McKnight's Pulse Rate Index (PRI) measures game quality by combining both teams' combined ratings with a margin multiplier. A 6-point margin earns a 0.75 multiplier — competitive but decisive. In 1918, both Englewood and Hyde Park were among the highest-rated programs in the country, producing a raw PRI score of 168.29, the highest in our database of 5 million+ games. This game ranks #1 not just because it was close, but because two genuinely elite programs met at peak form.

Historical Context

⚔️ World War I & the Tank Corps
Hyde Park entered the 1918 season as the preseason favorite for the Chicago Public League title. However, in the weeks before the Englewood game, seven of Hyde Park's key players were called up for active military service with the U.S. Army Tank Corps. The team that took the field on November 23rd was significantly depleted from the unit that had started the season.
🦠 The Spanish Influenza Epidemic
The 1918 Chicago Public League season was severely disrupted by the Spanish influenza pandemic, which killed an estimated 50 million people worldwide. Several weekend game dates were canceled entirely by the school board. The Englewood-Hyde Park game had originally been scheduled for late October but was postponed due to the epidemic, finally being played on November 23rd.
🏈 The Rivalry
Englewood and Hyde Park had been playing annual football games since 1889, making theirs one of the longest-running high school rivalries in the country. By 1918 the series had been contested nearly 30 times. The two schools dominated the Cook County and Chicago Public League throughout the 1890s and 1900s, regularly attracting crowds of 3,000 to 5,000 spectators. Their annual clash was considered the Chicago equivalent of the Harvard-Yale game by their respective student bodies.

The Game

Approximately 3,000 spectators attended the game at Normal Ball Park — an unusual venue, as the rivalry had traditionally been staged at the University of Chicago's Marshall Field and later Stagg Field. Cold and misty conditions reduced visibility across the field.

Englewood scored the game's only touchdown in the third quarter. Charles Palmer, Englewood's standout halfback, was the team's primary offensive threat. Hyde Park's captain Francis Mooney was recognized as the defensive standout for his side, keeping the margin from widening despite his team's depleted roster.

Despite being heavily undermanned, Hyde Park put up a creditable fight. The final score of 6-0 — considered a narrow margin in the higher-scoring football of that era — was described at the time as reflecting well on both programs given the circumstances.

Englewood went on to claim the 1918 Chicago Public League championship. Charles Palmer later became Englewood's head football coach in 1927 and also played professionally.
